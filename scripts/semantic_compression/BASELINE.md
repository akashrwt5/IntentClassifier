## What each row may be used for

- **`dev_hard`** — PRIMARY. Clean rows. This is the baseline P2-P8 is judged against.
- **`dev_near`** — Memorisation. Regression detection only.
- **`holdout_honest`** — The parent set. Reported only for continuity with earlier numbers.
- **`leakage_guard`** — Independent cross-check, built separately from the train/holdout split.
- **`semantic_holdout_2`** — Second independent cross-check.
  - 10 row(s) excluded: gold label(s) `Cmd.SendMessage - no`, `Cmd.SendMessage - yes` are not among the head's classes, so no model can score them.
- **`paraphrase`** — Deep on a narrow slice of the taxonomy. Never read as an overall figure.
- **`benchmark_250`** — B14: 85.5% leaked, 10 of 57 intents. Reported to show what it is worth.

## How much of the old number was memorisation

`dev_near` scores **0.8889** and `dev_hard` **0.8327** — a gap of **+0.0562** (95% CI ±0.0352, 3.1 SE). The two sets are disjoint, so this is a two-proportion comparison, not McNemar.

`holdout_honest` sits between them at 0.8578, which is what a 45% / 55% blend of the two should look like. That figure was never wrong — it was answering a different question than the one it was being asked. **`dev_hard` is the baseline from here.**


## Per-row predictions

`baseline_predictions.csv` holds one row per scored utterance: instrument, text, gold, prediction, correct. McNemar's test compares *which* rows two models get right, which cannot be recovered from two accuracy figures — so this file, not the table above, is what makes P2 comparable with P1.
